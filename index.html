Saeed Razavi

Hi, I'm Saeed! I'm a first-year Ph.D. student at the Australian National University, where I'm fortunate to be advised by Prof. Liang Zheng. My research focuses on generative models for visual data, particularly diffusion and flow-based models. I'm broadly interested in representation learning and its role in image synthesis, with the goal of building generative models that are more efficient, controllable, and reliable.

Before my Ph.D., I worked on medical image analysis at the RIML Lab, Sharif University of Technology, with Prof. Mohammad Hossein Rohban, and at TU Munich with Prof. Nassir Navab.

Research interests

  • Diffusion and flow-based generative models
  • Representation learning and conditioning for image synthesis

Publications

  1. arXiv 2026

    Representation Conditioning for Generation

    Saeed Razavi, Xingjian Leng, Liang Zheng

    arXiv project page code bibtex

    We propose RECON, a simple way to give the later layers of a diffusion transformer direct access to semantic features from a pretrained vision encoder. At inference, the model's own projection head stands in for the encoder, so RECON adds no extra parameters while making training converge much faster.

  2. NeurIPS 2025

    Navigating the MIL Trade-Off: Flexible Pooling for Whole Slide Image Classification

    Hossein Jafarinia, Danial Hamdi, Amirhossein Alamdar, Elahe Zahiri, Soroush Vafaie Tabar, Alireza Alipanah, Nahal Mirzaie, Saeed Razavi, Amir Najafi, Mohammad Hossein Rohban

    paper code bibtex

    We show that pooling in multiple-instance learning trades off stability against sensitivity, and propose Maxsoft, a pooling function that lets us control this trade-off for each task. Together with a simple augmentation for robustness, it gives strong whole-slide image classification even with little data.

  3. ECCV 2024

    Snuffy: Efficient Whole Slide Image Classifier

    Hossein Jafarinia, Alireza Alipanah, Saeed Razavi, Nahal Mirzaie, Mohammad Hossein Rohban

    paper arXiv code bibtex

    We propose Snuffy, an efficient sparse transformer for classifying whole-slide pathology images, and prove that despite its sparsity it remains a universal approximator. It achieves strong results while staying cheap to train.

Experience

  • Research Intern, Technical University of Munich Apr 2024 – Jan 2025

    Remote · with Prof. Nassir Navab

    I worked on medical image classification guided by concepts, i.e., predefined, human-interpretable attributes of an image. We used these concepts as a prior at three levels of granularity, depending on how much annotation is available, and proposed a contrastive, prototype-based objective that organizes image features around these concepts within and across classes.
